Okay so where do I even start with this game. I absolutely love this game, it is genuinely baffling to me how this game came out in the 1996, and the remake in 2005. The remake, despite coming out in 2005 holds up magnificently to this day, despite the fact that the original came out in 1996! It’s brilliant.

I finished the game clocking in about 22 hours total playtime on Steam. Not much of that was time spent AFK but a lot of it was spent lost and backtracking especially starting out. 
![Re1_result_screen_jill.jpeg](/images/Re1_result_screen_jill.jpeg)

Stats in order from left to right: 
- Play Time: 10:14:21
- Creatures Killed: 092
- Bullets used: 329
- Ink ribbons used: 28
- Healing items used: 30

Judging by the playtime on the result screen, I lost about 10 hours of progress to deaths. Which is to be expected I suppose. Though it did not feel like that, deaths are punishing but the bulk of the time spent on your first play through is going to be in figuring out everything. The backtracking can seem annoying and time consuming but knowing exactly where you need to go and what to do helps speed up everything 10 fold, and something that took me an hour or more to progress through ended up taking me 5 minutes to redo the second time. So with that being said, I actually really like the save system in this game. Having to be methodical with when you set your checkpoints makes sure you really have to understand everything and it’s fun finding the most efficient way of tackling an area as you struggle to get through. The game on normal difficulty gives you more than enough Ink Ribbons that you likely will be able to save whenever you want however saving them is critical as they are very scarce within each of the areas. If you save too early within an area you risk having to redo much of it because you tend to only get a stack or two of ink ribbons in each area at least on normal.

# Miscellaneous thoughts

Having beaten the game with Jill, I can only speak on how Jill’s route functions for now, though I do plan on returning to the game after finishing the rest, as is part of the challenge (see: [Resident Evil](/resident-rvil)). Of course I played as Jill, not only because it seems she’s the recommended character to start of with due to her lock pick, but also because—I love when games give me the option to play as a woman, it’s unironically an important thing to me and any game that allows me to do that gets a *big* plus in my book.


I do have some notes that I’ve written while playing, just random thoughts I had:

### I love how smart this game made me feel
The puzzles aren’t necessarily difficult or anything but they are very cleverly designed in a way that makes you just feel smart especially when you figure it out super quickly. Most of the solutions are quite obvious and the puzzles are generally constrained to their respective rooms and certain parts of areas, you don’t need to backtrack to the mansion after entering the courtyard in order to solve a crazy puzzle spanning multiple areas or anything like that. Which I find very nice.

I will say though, in the residence the backtracking really got to me for a bit, having to go back and forth up and down and all of that. And dying to Neptune was, really annoying but it wasn’t so bad the second time around as I already knew what to do where to go etc. 

One “puzzle” that seemed annoying but is quite manageable was the puzzle with the nitrogen capsule. 

![Nitrogen_capsule_re1.jpeg](/images/Nitrogen_capsule_re1.jpeg)


The item description says: it looks like it could explode just from *running*. The game also fails to mention that getting hit will also trigger the explosion (which is an instant game over). Thankfully this “puzzle” was actually very easy in practice, as I’d already killed all the enemies that could’ve been a problem so it was just about walking there, and paying attention to item descriptions!

### The door animations are annoying
They are cool and I understand that they were necessarily to hide loading times and stuff on the PS1, but they’re very long and can get annoying when you’re backtracking.

###
I really like that for most puzzles interacting with something doesn’t give you the prompt to use an item in your inventory, instead you must go to your inventory and use the correct item. It prevents you from just mindlessly spamming interact and lucking your way out through puzzles.

### Controls are good
Surprisingly good, with the “arrange” control scheme you get to use both the original tank controls and modern controls which were ported over beautifully. Hallways and stuff aren’t as bad as you’d expect with the modern controls as you keep moving in the direction you were moving in when they do change so long as you hold the stick in the same position.

Of course the option to use the tank controls on the D-PAD is also a great addition as it helps out sometimes, I didn’t make much use of it but I did use it while delivering the nitrogen capsule and to get through some sections with lots of camera angle switching.

### The spiders made my skin crawl
Scary. Me no like. So is good because horror game.

### Game is surprisingly long
Every time I felt that “oh so this has got to be the last area” there was always another one! Nothing to complain about obviously all of the areas are designed great and were very fun but man did I not expect this game to be as long as it is. It’s very impressive for a game originally released in 1996.
